Is this AI answer really true?

AI assistants often just agree with what you ask. Entrelinhas checks every claim in an answer against official sources, news and scientific articles found live with Tavily and read by a model through Bifrost. For each claim you see whether independent sources agree, whether the information is still current, and exactly where it comes from.

      How it works

      1. 1

        Split and flag

        The answer becomes atomic claims. Before searching, each one is flagged by how fast it goes stale: prices in about a month, software versions in a quarter, company facts in six months, laws and official rules in a year.

      2. 2

        Gather many voices

        Tavily searches official sites, the news (Brazilian outlets for Portuguese claims) and, for health and science, scientific articles. Up to five sources of different types; never two from one site; blogs last.

      3. 3

        Read them together

        One model call through Bifrost reads every source for a claim, knowing today's date: does it support or contradict? Every quote it returns is checked against the source text.

      4. 4

        Weigh and score

        Copies count once, official and scientific sources weigh most. Each claim gets a verdict and a freshness score: how recent the sources are × how many of them agree.

      5. 5

        Give the right answer

        A final call writes the answer again using only verified quotes, with numbered citations, next to what the AI said.

      Not just agreeing with you

      Chatbots tend to confirm what you ask. Entrelinhas looks for independent sources that support or contradict the claim, weighted by type: official and scientific 3, news 2, reference 1.5, other sites 1, blogs a quarter.

      Measured on real claims

      On 15 real claims with known answers: 11 exact verdicts, 15 of 15 in the right direction, and no outdated claim ever confirmed as true.

      Built for Portuguese

      Brazilian money and numbers (R$ 1.234,56, “5 mil”), qualifiers (“a partir de”, “cerca de”), “atualmente”, day/month dates and slang (“9 pau”, “50 conto”, “zap”) are read the way a Brazilian means them. Selic, LGPD or Anatel send the search to the right Brazilian authority.

      Resilient by design

      Every model call goes through the Bifrost gateway with a backup model. When the main model hits a rate limit, Bifrost answers from the backup; the report shows which model answered each step.

      How to read the results

      Source agreement and freshness are deterministic indexes, not probabilities of truth. Independent sources can share the same mistake, and a recent page can still be wrong.
