Fact-checking for AI answers · English and Portuguese

Is this AI answer really true?

AI assistants often just agree with what you ask. Entrelinhas checks every claim in an answer against official sources, news and scientific articles found live with Tavily and read by a model through Bifrost. For each claim you see whether independent sources agree, whether the information is still current, and exactly where it comes from.

Stale-risk triage

Before searching, each claim is flagged by how likely it is to have gone out of date, and why.

    AI gateway · Bifrost

    Every model call goes through Bifrost. It routes to the main model and, if that model fails or hits a rate limit, retries a backup model on its own: no retry code in Entrelinhas.

    How it works

    1. 1

      Split and categorize

      The answer becomes atomic claims. Each gets a category with its own validity window: prices 30 days, API versions 90, companies 180, regulations 365.

    2. 2

      Gather diverse sources

      Tavily searches the web, the news, official sites and, for science and health claims, scientific articles. Up to five sources of different types are read, never two from the same site; blogs come last.

    3. 3

      Read them together

      One model call through Bifrost reads all sources for a claim: same object? supports or contradicts? It knows today's date and Portuguese conventions.

    4. 4

      Weigh and explain

      Deterministic rules group copies, weigh source types, compute agreement and freshness, and give a verdict you can audit source by source.

    Not just agreeing with you

    An AI tends to confirm what you ask. Entrelinhas looks for independent official, news and scientific sources that support or contradict the claim, weighted by type. Ten copies of one blog post count once and weigh almost nothing.

    Freshness that refuses to guess

    Only a date tied to the specific fact feeds the freshness score. A page's publish date is shown separately, with low confidence.

    Portuguese-aware

    Brazilian numbers and money (R$ 1.234,56, “5 mil”), qualifiers (“a partir de”, “até”, “cerca de”), payment conditions (“no Pix”, “12x sem juros”), change words (“deixou de”, “entrou em vigor”), colloquial time (“ano que vem”) and day/month dates are read the way a Brazilian means them. Terms like Selic, LGPD or Anatel send the search to the right Brazilian authority.

    Traceable

    Every verification is stored with its sources, model calls and search operations, so any verdict can be checked later.

    How to read the results

    Source agreement and freshness are deterministic indexes, not probabilities of truth. Independent sources can share the same mistake, and a recent page can still be wrong.
